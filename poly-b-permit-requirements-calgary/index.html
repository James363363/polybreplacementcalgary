Does Replacing Poly-B Pipe in Calgary Require a Permit?

Short answer: yes. If you're replacing Poly-B water lines anywhere behind a Calgary wall, the City treats that as altering your plumbing system — which means a permit and inspections, not a quiet weekend project. Here's exactly what's required, who's responsible for it, and what it actually costs.

Why a Repipe Counts as "Altering" Your Plumbing System

It can feel like overkill to need a permit for something as routine as swapping out old pipe for new. But the City of Calgary's plumbing bylaw doesn't ask how big the job is — it asks whether the plumbing system itself is being constructed, extended, or altered. Replacing Poly-B supply lines that run inside your walls or ceilings meets that bar every time, because you're changing the pipe material, the fittings, and the connections that make up the system, even if the new pipe ends up running to the exact same fixtures.

Compare that to work that doesn't need a permit: swapping a faucet for an identical one, replacing a showerhead, or clearing a slow drain. Those are maintenance on parts that are still accessible and unchanged in configuration. A Poly-B repipe is the opposite — it's concealed work that changes the system, which is exactly the category the permit rule exists for.

Who's Responsible for Pulling the Permit

Hiring a licensed plumber

Your contractor pulls the permit as a normal part of the job. It's built into how a legitimate Calgary plumbing company operates — if a quote doesn't mention a permit for a full repipe, that's worth asking about directly.

Doing it yourself

The City allows homeowners to pull their own permit and do their own plumbing work, but only if you own the home, live in it, and you're the one physically doing the work — not hiring someone else under your name.

Already covered up?

If new pipe went in behind drywall without a permit, a homeowner can't retroactively self-permit it. A licensed plumbing contractor has to get involved to inspect or open the wall back up.

For most Calgary homeowners repiping an entire house, hiring a licensed plumber who handles the permit as part of the job is the simplest path — one less thing to track down while you're also coordinating the work itself.

What the Permit Actually Costs

Based on the City of Calgary's current fee schedule, a homeowner plumbing permit is $112, plus a 4% Safety Codes Council fee — a total of about $116.50. That's a modest line item next to the overall cost of a full Poly-B repipe, and when you hire a licensed contractor it's typically folded into the quote rather than billed to you separately. City fees are set independently and can change, so if you're planning far ahead, it's worth confirming the current number rather than assuming it's static.

The Inspection Process, in Plain Terms

A permit isn't just a piece of paper — it comes with two required inspection checkpoints:

  • Rough-in inspection: happens after the new pipe and fittings are run but before any drywall goes back up, so the inspector can actually see the work. Scheduling this correctly means walls stay open exactly as long as they need to and no longer.
  • Final inspection: confirms everything's been closed up correctly and the system is functioning as it should, once the job is complete.
  • Paper trail for resale: a permitted, inspected repipe gives you a documented record that a home inspector, buyer, or your insurer can actually verify — something an unpermitted job can never offer, no matter how well the work was done.

None of this needs to slow your project down. A crew that repipes Poly-B homes regularly plans the rough-in and final inspection windows into the schedule from the start, so the permit process runs in step with the work instead of holding it up.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit if I'm only replacing one section of pipe, not the whole system?

Usually yes. The City of Calgary's rule isn't about how much pipe you're touching — it's about whether you're altering the plumbing system. Replacing a run of Poly-B that's hidden inside a wall counts as an alteration even if it's just one section, because you're changing the pipe material and the connections behind the drywall. A like-for-like swap of an exposed, accessible part (say, a shutoff valve in the same spot) generally doesn't need one, but anything concealed almost always does.

Who actually pulls the permit — me or my plumber?

If you're hiring a licensed plumbing contractor, they pull the permit as a normal part of the job — that's standard practice and one of the things you're paying for. The City does let homeowners pull their own permit and do their own plumbing work, but only if you own the home, live in it yourself, and you're doing the work personally rather than hiring someone else to do it under your permit.

What happens if the work gets covered up without a permit?

This is the situation to avoid. If new pipe goes in behind drywall without a permit and inspection, the City won't let a homeowner retroactively self-permit that work — a licensed plumbing contractor has to get involved to verify or open up the wall so it can be properly inspected, and the homeowner is still on the hook for that cost. It's also a real problem at resale, since a home inspector or buyer's lawyer can ask for permit records on any repiping work.

Does pulling a permit slow down my repipe?

It adds required inspection steps — typically a rough-in inspection before walls are closed up, and a final inspection once everything's finished — but it doesn't have to add much real time. A crew that repipes Poly-B regularly builds those inspection windows into the schedule from day one, so the permit process runs alongside the job rather than stalling it.

How much does a Calgary plumbing permit actually cost?

As of the City of Calgary's current fee schedule, a homeowner plumbing permit runs $112 plus a 4% Safety Codes Council fee, for a total of about $116.50. That's a small line item next to the overall cost of a repipe, and if you're hiring a licensed contractor it's typically rolled into their quote rather than something you pay separately. Fees are set by the City and can change, so confirm the current number if it's been a while since you last checked.
