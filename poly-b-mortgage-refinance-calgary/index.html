Financing & Poly-B

Worried Poly-B Pipe Will Sink Your Mortgage or Refinance?

If you're trying to buy, refinance, or renew a mortgage on a Calgary home with Poly-B pipe, the pipe itself usually isn't the dealbreaker — what actually trips people up is insurance and the appraisal. Here's how mortgage lenders, insurers, and appraisers really treat Poly-B, and what to ask before you assume you're stuck.

The Short Answer: Poly-B Itself Rarely Blocks Financing Directly

There's no Alberta-wide rule that says a bank has to decline a mortgage or refinance just because a home has Poly-B pipe. In practice, the pipe material almost never shows up as its own line item on a lender's checklist. What actually causes problems is what Poly-B triggers downstream: trouble getting home insurance bound at a reasonable price, and an appraiser or lender who's cautious about an aging system they can see.

That distinction matters, because it changes what you should actually be doing to protect your financing — not panicking about the pipe itself, but getting ahead of the insurance and paperwork side of things before your lender asks.

Why Home Insurance Is the Real Gatekeeper

Almost every mortgage lender in Canada requires proof of active home insurance before they'll fund a purchase, and most require it to stay in force for the life of the loan — it's a standard condition on the loan itself, not something specific to Poly-B. So the practical risk isn't "will my lender reject Poly-B," it's "will an insurer bind coverage on this home, and at what price."

As covered in more detail on our home insurance page, Alberta insurers handle Poly-B very differently from one company to the next. Some don't ask about pipe material at all. Others apply a modest surcharge, add a plumbing-specific exclusion, or in some cases decline to write a new policy until the pipe is replaced or scheduled for replacement. If you land with an insurer in that last group and can't get coverage bound in time, that's what actually stalls a closing or a refinance — not a direct lender rule about Poly-B.

Refinancing: What the Appraisal Might Flag

A purchase mortgage and a refinance work a little differently here. Most refinances involve a fresh appraisal of the home, and an appraiser walking through your basement or utility room may note visible Poly-B pipe in their report — the same way they'd note an aging furnace, an outdated electrical panel, or a roof nearing the end of its life. It's a condition-of-the-home note, not an automatic red flag.

What happens with that note depends on your specific lender's internal risk guidelines and, again, your insurance situation. Some lenders won't blink at it. Others may ask a few follow-up questions or want to see that you have insurance confirmed before they'll finalize. The best move is telling your mortgage broker upfront that the home has Poly-B, rather than letting it come up as a surprise mid-appraisal.

Using a Refinance to Pay for the Repipe

If you're refinancing anyway — or considering a home equity line of credit — rolling the cost of a full Poly-B replacement into that financing is a common approach for Calgary homeowners, rather than paying the whole project out of pocket. Spreading the cost into your mortgage means you're not stuck finding several thousand dollars in cash at once just to protect your insurability and resale value down the road.

Whichever way you fund it, ask your plumber for a City of Calgary plumbing permit and a paid invoice once the work is done. That paperwork means you'll have documentation ready the next time an insurer, appraiser, or future buyer's lender asks whether the Poly-B is actually gone — a verbal "it's been replaced" rarely satisfies any of them on its own. What a Handful of Lenders May Ask For

None of this is universal — it varies by lender and can change as internal guidelines are updated. Here's the general pattern:

Generally not required

A specific written policy that names Poly-B and requires its removal before closing. Most Calgary mortgages fund on Poly-B homes without this ever coming up directly.

Sometimes requested

Proof of active, bound home insurance before funding; occasionally a request for a plumbing inspection, a documented replacement, or a holdback on a refinance if the appraiser flags visible deterioration.

The practical takeaway is the same one that applies to insurance: don't assume, ask. A five-minute call to your mortgage broker about how they and their usual lenders treat Poly-B can save you a surprise later in the process.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will having Poly-B pipe stop me from getting a mortgage in Calgary?

Not directly — there's no Alberta-wide lender rule that says Poly-B pipe alone disqualifies a mortgage application. The real chokepoint is usually insurance: almost every lender requires proof of active home insurance before they'll fund or renew a mortgage, and if an insurer won't bind coverage on a Poly-B home (or only offers it with a heavy surcharge), that can stall the financing indirectly. Some individual lenders may also ask questions or want documentation once they know a home has Poly-B, but this varies lender to lender rather than being a blanket rule.

Does refinancing a home with Poly-B pipe require an appraisal?

Most refinances do require a fresh appraisal, and an appraiser walking through your home may note visible Poly-B pipe in their report, the same way they'd note an aging furnace or roof. Whether that affects your approval depends on your specific lender's risk tolerance and your insurance situation — it isn't an automatic decline, but it's worth being upfront with your mortgage broker about it before the appraisal happens rather than being surprised partway through.

Can I refinance my home specifically to pay for a Poly-B replacement?

Yes, this is a common and reasonable use of refinancing — many Calgary homeowners roll a repipe into a refinance or a home equity line of credit rather than paying out of pocket. Talk to your lender about using the funds for a documented, permitted repipe; keeping the plumbing permit and paid invoice afterward also helps with insurance and any future resale.

Do I have to replace Poly-B before a lender will approve my mortgage or refinance?

Usually not as a strict across-the-board rule, but it depends on the lender and your insurance status. If your insurer won't cover the home as-is, some lenders will treat that as a barrier since they require proof of insurance to close. A handful of lenders may ask for a professional inspection or proof of a planned replacement, especially on an older or heavily Poly-B home. Ask your mortgage broker directly rather than assuming either way — the answer isn't the same at every institution.

What should I ask my mortgage broker or lender directly?

Ask whether they require proof of home insurance before funding, whether Poly-B specifically comes up in their underwriting or appraisal review, and whether a documented repipe (permit plus paid invoice from a licensed plumber) would help your file. These are the same questions worth asking your insurance broker, since insurance and financing are closely linked for a Poly-B home.
