Poly-B in Cold Weather

Why Cold Hits Aging Poly-B Differently

Every water pipe is vulnerable to freezing if it's exposed to enough cold for long enough — water expands as it turns to ice, and that expansion can split a pipe wall or blow apart a fitting regardless of what the pipe is made of. That part isn't specific to Poly-B.

What is specific to Poly-B is the condition it's already in by the time a Calgary winter tests it. Years of chlorine exposure in municipal tap water gradually make the plastic more brittle from the inside out, which is the same mechanism behind the cracking and fitting failures covered in our Poly-B warning signs guide. Cold temperatures compound that: plastic naturally gets stiffer and less flexible as it cools, the same way a garden hose left outside overnight gets harder to coil. An already-brittle pipe that's also stiffened by cold has less give left to absorb the pressure of ice forming inside it — so where a newer, more flexible line might tolerate a freeze without damage, an aging Poly-B fitting is more likely to be the point that gives.

Where the Risk Actually Concentrates

Garages & unheated additions

Any pipe run through a space without reliable heat is exposed to full outdoor cold for as long as the space stays cold.

Exterior walls

Pipes routed close to an outside wall get less benefit from the home's interior heat than pipes buried deeper in the structure.

Crawlspaces & rim joists

Drafty, poorly insulated basement rim joist areas are a classic cold spot, especially in older Calgary housing stock.

Calgary's chinooks make this worse in a specific way: the city doesn't just get cold, it swings from deep freezes to sudden thaws and back again. Those repeated freeze-thaw cycles put fittings through expansion and contraction over and over across a single winter, rather than one steady cold stretch — which is a different kind of stress on already-brittle Poly-B joints than a straightforward cold snap.

What Actually Helps Before the Next Cold Snap

The City of Calgary publishes seasonal reminders on frozen pipe prevention because it's a real, recurring local problem, not just a plumbing-company talking point. The basics apply to any home, but matter more with aging Poly-B in the mix: keep the areas around exposed pipes — garages, crawlspaces, under sinks on exterior walls — as warm as you reasonably can, and open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during a hard cold snap so household heat can reach the pipes. During an extended deep freeze, let a faucet run at a slow, steady drip on the affected line — moving water resists freezing far better than standing water, and it relieves pressure if ice does start to form.

Know where your main shutoff valve is before you need it. If a pipe does freeze, being able to shut off water fast can be the difference between a contained problem and a flooded basement the moment it thaws.

If a Pipe Has Already Frozen

Shut off your main valve first — a frozen section may have already cracked, and it won't show as a leak until it thaws and the water starts moving again. Don't apply direct heat yourself with an open flame or a heat gun; aging Poly-B fittings can be damaged by uneven or excessive heat in ways that create a new leak rather than fixing the freeze. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Poly-B pipe actually more likely to freeze than copper or PEX?

Any water pipe can freeze and split if it's exposed to sustained cold with no protection — that part isn't unique to Poly-B. What is specific to Poly-B is that decades of chlorine exposure already make the plastic more brittle than it was when installed, and cold temperatures make plastic stiffer and less able to flex. Combine an aging, already-brittle pipe with a stretch of deep cold, and it has less give to absorb the pressure of ice forming inside it than a newer line would.

Which pipes in my house are most at risk during a cold snap?

Anything running through an unheated or poorly insulated space: pipes in the garage, along an exterior wall, in a crawlspace, or near a drafty basement rim joist. Interior pipes running through heated living space are much lower risk, since they're surrounded by warm air on all sides.

Does leaving a faucet dripping during a cold snap actually help?

Yes. A slow, steady drip keeps water moving through the line, and moving water is much harder to freeze solid than standing water. It also relieves pressure buildup in the pipe if ice does start to form somewhere along the line, which reduces the chance of a split.

What should I do if I turn on a tap and nothing comes out during a cold snap?

Shut off the main water valve first, in case the frozen section has already cracked and is waiting to leak the moment it thaws. Then call a plumber rather than applying direct heat yourself — an open flame or a heat gun aimed at aging Poly-B fittings can do more damage than the freeze itself. A professional can safely locate and thaw the frozen section, or replace it if it's already split.

Does home insurance cover damage from a frozen and burst Poly-B pipe?

Coverage for sudden water damage from a burst pipe is standard on most Canadian home policies, but many insurers have specific exclusions or extra scrutiny for Poly-B specifically, and some policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent freezing (like maintaining heat in the home) for a claim to be honoured. It's worth checking your specific policy's wording — see our home insurance and Poly-B guide for what to ask your broker.
