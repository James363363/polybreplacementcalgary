Is Poly-B Pipe Covered by Home Insurance in Alberta?

Short answer: replacing Poly-B before it fails is generally not something insurance pays for — but water damage caused by a Poly-B pipe that actually bursts or leaks is typically covered as a peril under a standard homeowner policy. Here's how that distinction plays out in real Calgary policies, and what to ask your broker before you assume either way.

A quick note before we go further: this page is general information for Calgary homeowners, not insurance or legal advice. Insurance policies, underwriting rules, and premiums vary by insurer, by policy, and by your individual circumstances. Always confirm coverage details, exclusions, and requirements directly with your own insurance broker or provider before making a decision.

Replacing Poly-B proactively: usually not a covered cost

If you're calling your insurer to ask "will you pay to replace my Poly-B pipe before it breaks," the honest answer for most Alberta homeowners is no. Proactive Poly-B replacement is generally treated the same way as replacing an aging roof or an old furnace: it's a home-maintenance and upgrade expense, not a covered loss. Insurance is built to respond to sudden, unexpected events — not to fund preventing one that hasn't happened yet.

That said, this is exactly where the confusion lives, because the pipe material itself and the damage it can cause are two very different things in the eyes of a policy.

Water damage from a Poly-B failure: often covered as a peril

If a Poly-B fitting cracks and floods a basement, or a pipe behind a wall lets go overnight, that's typically a different story. Sudden water damage from a burst or leaking pipe is a standard covered peril under most Alberta homeowner policies, regardless of what the pipe is made of. The insurer isn't paying you to replace Poly-B — they're paying to repair the damage the failure caused: flooring, drywall, cabinets, and sometimes the plumbing repair itself.

The specifics — what's covered, what's excluded, deductibles, and whether gradual leaks are treated differently from sudden bursts — vary by insurer and by the exact wording of your policy. This is a case where reading your policy or calling your broker matters more than any general rule.

Generally treated as covered

Sudden water damage from a burst pipe, a failed fitting, or an active leak — subject to your policy's deductible and specific exclusions. Confirm with your broker.

Generally not covered

The cost of proactively replacing Poly-B pipe as preventive maintenance, before any failure or damage has occurred. Most insurers treat this as a home upgrade, not a loss.

How Poly-B can affect your policy, premium, or renewal

Some Alberta insurers have started asking about plumbing material — including Poly-B — at renewal time or when underwriting a new policy. What happens next varies a lot by company:

  • Some insurers don't ask at all and treat Poly-B homes the same as any other.
  • Some may apply a modest premium surcharge once they know a home has Poly-B.
  • Some may add a specific exclusion for plumbing-related water damage claims.
  • In some cases, an insurer may decline to write a new policy, or ask for proof of replacement (or a scheduled replacement) before they'll bind coverage.

None of this is universal, and it can change from one renewal to the next as insurers update their underwriting guidelines. The practical takeaway isn't to panic — it's to ask.

Questions worth asking your broker directly

  • "Will you cover water damage caused by a Poly-B pipe failure?" Get this answered in plain terms so you know exactly what you're protected against before anything happens.
  • "Does having Poly-B affect my premium or my renewal?" Some policies are unaffected; others carry a surcharge or exclusion — better to know now than to find out at claim time.
  • "Will you require proof of replacement to renew my policy?" A handful of insurers are starting to ask for this. If yours is one of them, you'll want to plan ahead rather than be caught off guard.
  • "If I replace it, do I need to provide a permit or invoice to get any premium benefit?" Knowing this upfront means you keep the right paperwork from day one instead of chasing it down later.

Why documentation matters more than a verbal assurance

If you do replace your Poly-B system, telling your insurer "it's done" usually isn't enough on its own. What insurers, mortgage lenders, and future home buyers actually want to see is paperwork: a City of Calgary plumbing permit for the work, and a paid invoice from a licensed plumber showing the scope of the repipe. That combination is the closest thing to proof that the work was done properly and inspected — and it's what you'll be asked for if you ever want a premium adjustment, need to satisfy a lender, or are selling a home where a buyer's insurer is asking questions.

It's worth keeping digital and paper copies of both documents somewhere easy to find — you may need them years after the work is finished, at a renewal, an appraisal, or a sale.

FAQ: Poly-B and home insurance in Alberta

Will my insurance premium go up because I have Poly-B?

It depends entirely on your insurer and your policy — some don't ask about pipe material at all, others may apply a small surcharge or a plumbing-specific exclusion once they know a home has Poly-B. There's no single Alberta-wide rule. The only way to know for sure is to ask your broker directly what your specific policy does.

What happens if a Poly-B pipe bursts and floods my basement — is that covered?

In most standard homeowner policies, sudden water damage from a burst or leaking pipe is treated as a covered peril, regardless of what the pipe is made of — that's different from the cost of proactively replacing the pipe itself, which is generally not covered. But every policy has its own wording, limits, and exclusions, so confirm this with your broker rather than assuming.

Do I need to tell my insurer I have Poly-B?

Most policies require you to answer underwriting questions honestly, and some insurers now specifically ask about plumbing material at renewal or when you switch providers. Not disclosing something they've directly asked about can put a claim at risk. When in doubt, tell your broker and let them tell you what it means for your coverage.

Does replacing Poly-B lower my premium?

Some insurers offer a modest discount or remove a plumbing surcharge once Poly-B is fully replaced with PEX or copper, but this isn't universal and it isn't automatic — you'll typically need to show proof, like a permit and a paid invoice from a licensed plumber, and ask your broker to update your file.

Will my insurer require proof if I say I've replaced it?

Many will, especially for a premium adjustment or to remove a Poly-B flag from your file. A verbal assurance usually isn't enough — insurers, lenders, and home buyers alike tend to want a copy of the City of Calgary plumbing permit and a paid invoice from a licensed plumber as documentation that the work was actually done and inspected.
