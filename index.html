Why so many Calgary homes still have Poly-B

Polybutylene ("Poly-B") pipe was installed in an estimated 700,000+ Canadian homes between the late 1970s and mid-1990s because it was cheap and fast to install compared to copper. Calgary's building boom in that exact window means whole pockets of the city — bungalows in older SW and NW communities, split-levels in the NE, and townhome complexes across the city — were plumbed almost entirely in Poly-B. The problem showed up over time: the pipe reacts with chlorine and chloramine in treated municipal water, going brittle from the inside out, and the plastic fittings crack under normal water pressure. It doesn't fail on a schedule — some homes go 30 years with no issue, others get a burst line in year 15 — which is exactly why insurers and home inspectors flag it rather than ignore it.

What we actually replace it with

We re-pipe with cross-linked polyethylene (PEX), the current industry standard in Alberta. PEX doesn't degrade from chlorine exposure the way Poly-B does, it flexes instead of cracking in a Calgary winter freeze, and it comes with long manufacturer warranties (typically 25 years). Where a client specifically wants copper — some older-home buyers and condo boards do, for resale or code reasons — we quote that too. Either way, you get a written material spec before we start, not a surprise on the invoice.

How Poly-B replacement works, start to finish

  1. Free on-site assessment. We walk the home, confirm it's actually Poly-B (not PEX or copper, which get mistaken for it), and check accessible runs in the basement, crawlspace, and behind fixtures.
  2. Written quote with a fixed price and timeline. You'll know the total cost, the number of days on site, and exactly which drywall or ceiling areas need to be opened — before you commit to anything.
  3. Re-pipe day by day, water restored each night. We work zone by zone (main floor, then basement, then upper floor) so you're never without water overnight.
  4. Pressure test and inspection. Every new line is pressure-tested before we close up walls, and where required, the work is signed off to Alberta plumbing code.
  5. Patch and clean-up. We patch the drywall we cut (paint-ready, not painted) and leave the work areas broom-clean.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my Calgary home has Poly-B pipe?

Poly-B is a flexible grey (sometimes black or white) plastic pipe, usually stamped "PB2110" along its length, most common in Calgary homes built between 1978 and 1995. It's typically visible where pipes enter the water heater, furnace room, or under sinks. If you're not sure, we'll check for free during a quote visit.

How long does a full Poly-B replacement take?

Most Calgary bungalows and 2-storeys take 2-4 days for a full re-pipe with PEX; larger homes or homes with finished basements needing more drywall repair can take a week. We'll give you an exact timeline in your written quote before any work starts.

Will my water be shut off for days during replacement?

No. We restore water service at the end of each working day. You may be without water for a few hours at a time while we're actively cutting in new lines, but you won't go a night without water.

Does home insurance cover Poly-B replacement in Alberta?

Generally no — most Alberta insurers treat proactive Poly-B replacement as home maintenance, not a covered claim. Some insurers will reimburse damage caused by a Poly-B failure after the fact. See our full insurance guide for the details and how to ask your insurer directly.
