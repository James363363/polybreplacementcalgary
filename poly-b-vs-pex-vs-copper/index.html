The short answer: PEX is what most Calgary homes get today

When Poly-B fails or gets flagged by an inspector or insurer, homeowners usually expect three options on the table. In practice there are really only two: PEX and copper. Straight Poly-B-to-Poly-B replacement isn't something any licensed plumber offers anymore — the pipe hasn't been manufactured for residential use since the mid-1990s, it's no longer accepted under the Alberta Building Code, and there's simply no supply chain left for it.

Of the two materials still on the table, PEX is what the large majority of Calgary repipes use. It's flexible, faster to install, and generally less expensive than copper without sacrificing code compliance or reliability. Copper is a legitimate alternative — some buyers, older-home purists, and condo boards prefer it, and we're glad to quote it — but for most single-family repipes, PEX is the practical recommendation.

What each material actually means for your home

Poly-B

The material you're replacing. Grey/blue plastic pipe common in Calgary homes built 1978-1995. Its chlorine-brittleness and acetal-fitting failure mode make it a known liability — no longer installed by any licensed plumber.

PEX

The modern default. Flexible like Poly-B (fewer fittings, faster install) but built with a different plastic chemistry and typically paired with brass or copper fittings instead of the acetal ones that plagued Poly-B. Code-accepted and the standard recommendation for most Calgary repipes.

Copper

The premium legacy option. Rigid, requires more joints, costs more to install — but boasts a 50+ year track record and no plastic in the water line, which some buyers and condo boards specifically want.

Why some homeowners still request copper

Even though PEX is the practical, code-accepted, budget-friendly choice for most repipes, copper hasn't gone anywhere — and there are honest reasons some Calgary homeowners still ask for it:

  • Resale positioning for older, higher-end homes: some buyers in established Calgary neighbourhoods specifically look for copper, treating it as a mark of a "properly done" renovation.
  • Condo and townhouse board requirements: certain condo corporations specify copper (or a mix of copper stub-outs) in their building standards, particularly for shared-wall or common-element plumbing.
  • No plastic in the water line, period: a small number of homeowners simply prefer an all-metal system after their Poly-B experience, even knowing PEX doesn't share Poly-B's failure mechanism.

We quote both materials honestly and won't steer you toward the pricier option just to pad the invoice — if copper fits your situation, we'll tell you; if PEX is the smarter call for your budget, we'll tell you that too.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PEX going to fail like Poly-B did?

No — PEX doesn't share the specific failure mechanism that doomed Poly-B. Poly-B pipe went brittle from years of exposure to chlorinated municipal water, especially combined with the acetal (plastic) fittings that were commonly used with it. PEX is a different plastic chemistry, is almost always installed with brass or copper fittings instead of acetal, and has been the mainstream code-accepted repipe standard across Alberta for over two decades with a strong track record. No pipe material is risk-free forever, but PEX isn't following the same script Poly-B did.

Can you mix PEX and copper in the same repipe?

Yes, and it's actually common. A lot of Calgary repipes run PEX through the walls and attic where flexibility saves time and fittings, then transition to a short stub of copper at fixture hookups or where a condo board or inspector wants to see it. We'll walk you through where that makes sense for your home.

Which one should I choose for resale value?

Either one clears the Poly-B red flag for a buyer or their insurer, which is the part that actually matters for resale. Copper carries a slight prestige edge with some older-home buyers and certain condo boards, but a documented PEX repipe from a licensed plumber satisfies the vast majority of Calgary buyers and lenders just fine.

Is copper worth the extra cost?

It depends on your priorities. Copper typically lasts longer, has no plastic in the water line, and satisfies certain condo board specs — but it costs more up front and takes longer to install because of all the extra soldered joints. If budget and speed matter most, PEX is the practical choice. If you're set on copper for resale positioning or personal preference, we quote it the same as PEX.

Why can't I just replace Poly-B with new Poly-B?

Poly-B hasn't been manufactured for residential use in North America since the mid-1990s, and it's no longer accepted under the Alberta Building Code for new plumbing work. There's no supply chain for it anymore and no plumber who would install it even if there were — so every legitimate repipe quote you get will be PEX, copper, or a mix of the two.
